Bicycle Model Lab

Three cars get the same steering input at the same speed. The kinematic model assumes no tire slip. The dynamic models make lateral force from slip angle: first with linear tires, Fy=Cα, then with a Fiala brush tire that saturates at μFz.

Things to try

  • 5 m/sStep steer at low speed: all three paths overlap. The kinematic model is fine when lateral acceleration is small.
  • 30 m/sNow raise the speed. The kinematic car turns on a much tighter circle, instantly. The dynamic cars lag, overshoot slightly in yaw rate, and settle on a larger radius (understeer).
  • ramp 8°At 20 m/s the linear model draws a straight line of slope K on the handling diagram. The Fiala model bends upward as the front axle saturates (limit understeer), and ay caps near μ.
  • oversteerPush speed past vcrit: the linear model diverges and spins. With μ = 0.9 the Fiala model saturates the rear first and spins too.
  • sine 1.5 HzKinematic yaw rate follows δ with no phase lag. The dynamic models lag and lose amplitude. That is the yaw dynamics the kinematic model throws away.
  • μ 0.3On the neutral preset, step-steer: linear and Fiala split early, and the operating points slide onto the flat top of the tire curve.

Equations

Kinematic: no slip, wheel velocity along the wheel

Dynamic: lateral + yaw, vx constant

Tires: linear vs Fiala brush

Understeer gradient

Both dynamic models hold vx constant, ignore load transfer, and use the same μ on both axles. The linear model also uses small-angle kinematics, so its steady state matches the K formula exactly.
