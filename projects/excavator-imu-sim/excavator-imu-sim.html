How the automation works

Each link carries a simulated six-axis IMU. Gyro rates drift with an unknown constant bias; a two-state Kalman filter per axis fuses them with gravity-referenced angle measurements to estimate every link's pitch, roll, yaw and gyro bias — watch the bias readouts converge to the true values in the first seconds. Forward kinematics through the estimated pitches gives the bucket-edge position (the green crosshair against the true linkage).

The controller sees only these estimates. In automation it solves a 2×2 system each step: given your stick rate, it finds the boom and bucket rates that keep the edge on the cut level and hold the cutting angle, through rate limits and actuator lag. The gauge beside the machine shows the live depth to the target — green inside a ±5 cm band.
